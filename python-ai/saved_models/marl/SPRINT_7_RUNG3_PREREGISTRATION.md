## 1. Hypothesis under test (H2, from `SPRINT_7_R3_REPORT.md` §14)

> The binding constraint on risk-conditioned learning is not the *variance* of
> the actor gradient but its **composition**: high-risk decision entries are a
> 3.3–6.5% minority, the gradient restricted to them points the correct way in
> every arm, and the full gradient's direction is set by the ~95% low-risk
> remainder. Improving the estimator therefore improves a predominantly
> low-risk gradient.

## 2. The exact decomposition being measured

The production actor policy gradient at a checkpoint's own parameters is a sum
over decision entries (`mappo.py`, entropy term excluded because it is
advantage-independent):

```
g_full = -(1/D) * SUM_{j in decision}  A_j * grad log pi(a_j | s_j)
```

Partition the decision index set by the production high-risk rule into `hi` and
`lo`. Because `g` is a **linear** functional of the per-entry contributions:

```
g_full = g_hi + g_lo        EXACTLY, with the SAME 1/D normaliser in both terms
```

This identity holds regardless of the PPO ratio, and will be asserted
numerically at runtime (`|| g_full - (g_hi + g_lo) || / ||g_full|| < 1e-5`). It
is the whole basis of the rung: every quantity below is a function of the pair
`(g_hi, g_lo)` and the reference direction `g_synth`.

**Reference direction** `synth`, unchanged from Rung 2.75: `+1` on
MIGRATE_EDGE, `-1` on STAY, at high-risk decision entries, zero elsewhere;
rescaled to `g_full`'s L2 advantage mass over decision entries. It presupposes
that MIGRATE_EDGE is the correct high-risk action, which a training run cannot
know; it is a reference DIRECTION, not a target.

## 3. Definitions that MUST NOT change

Carried over verbatim; any deviation is a methodology change and would
invalidate comparison with Rungs 2.5/2.75/R3:

- high-risk rule for state-set scoring: `min(int(risk*5), 4) >= 3`
- high-risk rule inside the gradient probe: `risk > 0.50` (`HI_RISK`, as in
  `_diag_rung2_75_coherence.py`) — these two coexist in the existing machinery
  and I am **not** reconciling them; each is used exactly where it already was.
- decision rule: `mask.sum(-1) > 1`
- advantage: `agent.compute_gae(buf)`, then normalised over decision entries
  only, exactly as `mappo.py:360-366`
- RANDOM state set: uniform over legal actions, `--random-seed 31337`
- UNION state set: A0's states pooled with R2's, `ep_offset=1000`
- start ticks: `eval_starts(cfg_A0, 32, 20260825)`
- arms: A0 `mappo_A0_cpu_repro.pth`, R2 `mappo_R2_mc_target.pth`,
  R3 `R3_batch32.pth`

## 4. Measurement batch

**32 episodes**, not 8. `SPRINT_7_R3_REPORT.md` §13.2 established that the
8-episode buffer is underpowered: `real/shuffled` reverses the A0-vs-R2 rank
order between 8 and 32 episodes, and the shuffled null is verified to scale as
1/sqrt(N). This is the documented correction from the previous rung, applied
deliberately and declared here in advance rather than chosen after seeing
results.

## 5. State sources — three, deliberately

| source | actions are | ratio at own params | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| OWN | the arm's own stochastic samples | **exactly 1** | production-faithful; the only source where the vanilla-PG identity is exact |
| RANDOM | uniform-legal | not 1 | policy-independent; isolates state selection |
| UNION | A0-greedy pooled with R2-greedy | not 1 | on-manifold, symmetric, contains neither arm's R3-specific selection |

**Disclosed limitation, stated in advance.** On RANDOM and UNION the actions
were generated by a foreign behaviour policy, so the PPO ratio is not 1 and the
quantity computed there is a **counterfactual** gradient — "what the actor
gradient would be if these were the sampled entries" — not the production
update. I compute the vanilla-PG form on all three sources for comparability
and label RANDOM/UNION accordingly. OWN is the source that speaks to production;
RANDOM/UNION are the sources that speak to state-selection robustness. No
conclusion will be drawn from RANDOM/UNION alone.

## 7. Pre-registered criteria

### D1 — hypothesis (A): does the high-risk gradient contain useful directional signal?

- **D1a** `cos(g_hi, g_synth) > 0` for **all three** arms on **all three**
  sources (9/9 cells).
- **D1b** `cos(g_hi, g_synth)` exceeds the **cardinality-matched random-subset
  null** at one-sided `p < 0.05` in at least **7 of 9** cells.

**Null for D1b (item 7 of the brief):** draw `n_hi` decision entries uniformly
at random from ALL decision entries, compute the gradient of that subset with
the arm's own advantages, and take `cos(g_subset, g_synth)`. 200 draws. This
asks "is the high-risk set special, versus an arbitrary set of the same
cardinality?" It is a **different** null from Rung 2.75's, which permuted
advantage *values* while keeping the support fixed. Both are reported; D1b uses
this one.

**D1 passes iff D1a AND D1b.**

### D2 — hypothesis (B): is the high-risk gradient large enough to materially control the full update?

Three conditions, all of which must hold for D2 to be scored as
"g_hi does NOT control the update", i.e. **D2 pass = the update is dominated by
low-risk mass**:

- **D2a** high-risk share of decision entries `< 0.10` in all 9 cells.
- **D2b** `||g_hi|| / ||g_full|| < 0.50` in at least 7 of 9 cells.
- **D2c** removing `g_hi` barely moves the update direction:
  `cos(g_full, g_lo) > 0.90` in at least 7 of 9 cells.

D2c is the operative one. D2a is a fact about the data, D2b about magnitude,
D2c about whether the direction that PPO actually follows is set by the
low-risk bulk. **If D2c fails, `g_hi` already materially controls the update and
dilution is not the mechanism**, regardless of D2a/D2b.

### D3 — competing-gradient interference (must be distinguished from dilution)

- **D3** `cos(g_hi, g_lo) < -0.10` in at least 5 of 9 cells.

If D3 holds, the low-risk gradient actively **opposes** the high-risk one. That
is a different mechanism from dilution: up-weighting `g_hi` would then be
fighting a live counter-signal rather than merely amplifying a quiet one.

### D4 — does the decomposition explain the observed A0 → R2 → R3 ordering?

The behavioural fact to be explained (UNION risk response Δ, from
`SPRINT_7_R3_REPORT.md` §6): **R2 (+0.1579) > R3 (+0.1242) > A0 (+0.0191)**.

- **D4** at least one of `||g_hi||/||g_lo||`, `||g_hi||/||g_full||`, or
  `cos(g_full, g_synth)` reproduces the ordering **R2 > R3 > A0** on the OWN
  source.

D4 is the honesty check. A mechanism that is present in all arms equally cannot
explain why the arms differ, and in particular cannot explain the R3
degradation.

### D5 — analytical sensitivity (offline only; NOT applied to training)

For `w` in {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} form `g(w) = w*g_hi + g_lo` and report
`cos(g(w), g_synth)`, `cos(g(w), g(1))`, `||g(w)||/||g(1)||`. Also report the
exact analytic crossing point where the alignment numerator changes sign:

```
w* = - <g_lo, g_synth> / <g_hi, g_synth>
```

- **D5** "would materially change" is pre-defined as `cos(g(w), g(1)) < 0.90`;
  "would align better" as `cos(g(w), g_synth) > cos(g(1), g_synth) + 0.10`.

D5 is descriptive and does **not** gate the verdict. It exists to quantify how
much leverage a hypothetical re-weighting would have, so that a future rung is
not designed on an unquantified guess. **No weighting is applied to training in
this rung.**

## 8. Verdict rule — fixed now, applied without modification

| condition | verdict |
|---|---|
| D1 fails | **FALSIFIED** — the high-risk gradient carries no reliable directional signal |
| D1 passes, D3 passes | **COMPETING-GRADIENT INTERFERENCE** — reported separately, NOT as simple dilution |
| D1 passes, D3 fails, D2 passes | **SUPPORTED (simple dilution)** |
| D1 passes, D3 fails, D2 fails (esp. D2c) | **NOT SUPPORTED** — `g_hi` already controls the update, so dilution cannot be the constraint |
| any of the above, but D4 fails | append **"DOES NOT EXPLAIN R3"** and stop; the compositional hypothesis does not account for the degradation it was invoked to explain |

**GO for a future training rung requires D1 AND D2 AND D4.** Anything less is
NO-GO for a training rung. D5 magnitudes may inform a future design but cannot
create a GO.

I will not invent a new success metric after seeing the results. If the results
are interesting in a way these criteria do not capture, that goes in an
explicitly labelled exploratory section and does not touch the verdict.

## 9. Mechanism separation (item 9 of the brief) — assignment fixed in advance

| mechanism | the measurement that speaks to it | how it is kept distinct |
|---|---|---|
| **signal dilution** | `n_hi/n_dec`, `||g_hi||/||g_full||`, `cos(g_full, g_lo)` | dilution requires `g_hi` aligned AND small AND `g_full` ≈ `g_lo` |
| **estimator noise** | 8ep vs 32ep stability of every cosine; the cardinality-matched null's spread; cluster-bootstrap over the 32 episodes | a quantity that flips sign between buffers is noise, not mechanism |
| **policy-state selection** | OWN vs RANDOM vs UNION disagreement | if a conclusion holds only on OWN it is a selection artefact |
| **action-channel effects** | `cos(g_hi, g_synth_CLOUD)` alongside `cos(g_hi, g_synth)`, where `synth_CLOUD` is +1 on MIGRATE_CLOUD / −1 on STAY at high risk | tests whether the reference direction, not the gradient, is misspecified |

These four will not be conflated: each gets its own subsection and its own
numbers, and no single number is used as evidence for more than one.
